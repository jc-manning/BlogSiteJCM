The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction

Walter Benjamin’s The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction and it lines up with the videos I create. Most videos I make were never this one important/special object sitting in a room. In reality, they’re files. They’re supposed to get copied, compressed, sent around, watched on a phone, or what have you. Benjamin posits that aura is what withers and weakens in that process. “Aura”, being the work’s unique presence in time and space, like how famous art pieces have an intangible or unspoken energy behind it when seeing it in person. A photo can put anything you can imagine on your screen. A film can show a performance that an actor didn’t even do all at once. That’s useful, but you’re not there experiencing it first hand. The real question is, did you make your art knowing it was going to live as a copy?

Benjamin also says that art used to be more about ritual than about being on display. The piece/object just had to exist. A lot of artwork wasn’t even really meant to be looked at by everybody. Then that flips. Exhibition value can take over, and the work becomes made to be shown, moved around, put in front of people. Video/Film is where it gets obvious. The camera isn’t sitting in a theater with an actor. It breaks the performance into pieces, and the audience ends up looking with the camera. Because the shots keep changing, one can’t look the way you can look at a painting. One’s eye doesn’t get to stay on one image. Benjamin says that kind of watching is distracted, like how one drives down the road. You take it in while you’re already doing something else. However, Benjamin thought that film can still show you things that you miss in your regular-everyday-life. A random movement of a bird, a busy street, the crowd in a train station. Sometimes an edit does that and feels like it matters, sometimes it just turns into mundane consumable content.

I think the ending of the essay is what stuck with me most. Benjamin says fascism makes politics look like art. People express themselves but it doesn’t actually change the power situation/dynamic. At the worst end, destruction starts looking beautiful. I don’t think the answer is making work “about politics”. It’s moreso, what do the images do? Who gets to see it? How will it move? Can somebody you don’t agree take it the work use it against a perspective? If the piece is just trying to feel important or authentic, I don’t know if that’s enough.

The Telegarden

The Telegarden is a quiet, but radical work of early internet art because it protested the web’s need for speed. Goldberg put a robot in a live planter and invited strangers online to plant and water seeds via the robot, turning hardware and software into a tool of patience rather than efficiency. What formed is less a demo of a cool machine than it is a social experiment: online, thousands of strangers negotiated care and even grieved over plants seen only through a webcam. This piece questioned whether presence can survive mediation, and whether a commons can hold when the only shared labor is a click that says “water.” . It's is also encouraging people to go outside into a real garden, instead of wasting time in an online one.
